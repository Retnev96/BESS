    SUBMARINE FIRE CONTROL TRAINER

    BEARINGS ONLY

    Your sonar gives you bearings and nothing else. Work out where each contact is, which way it is heading and how fast, then put a torpedo on it.

    DIFFICULTY

    CONTACTS
    AUDIO

    The picture

    Bearing lines fan out from where your boat was when each bearing came in. The auto-tracker names contacts S1, S2… It will drop and swap tracks. Merge and split them so each track is one ship.

    The ruler

    Press R and drag from an old bearing line to a recent one. The ruler is the target's track. When every tick sits on its line and the dot stack stands straight, you have course, speed and range.

    The shot

    One leg cannot fix range. Turn 40 to 90 degrees, let new bearings build, refine the ruler, then press F. The torpedo runs out on your solution and its seeker does the rest.

    Mouse on the plot

    Wheel zooms. Right-drag or drag empty water pans. Click a bearing line to select its track. Drag a ruler end to slide it along the bearing lines; hold Shift to place it freely. Drag the middle of the ruler to shift the whole thing.

    How bearings-only TMA works

    A passive sonar hears a bearing, never a range. A ship on a steady course and speed crosses each successive bearing line at an even spacing in time. Lay a straight ruler across the lines so its tick marks, one per line, land on the lines they belong to.

    On a single own-ship leg there is a whole family of rulers that fit: near and slow, or far and fast. Changing course breaks the tie, because only the true range keeps fitting after your boat turns. That is why the OBSERVABILITY chip asks for a second leg.

    The dot stack plots each bearing's error against your solution over time. A vertical stack centred on zero means a good fit. Dots leaning one way mean the course or speed is off; a bend at one moment means the contact maneuvered or the tracker swapped ships there. Split the track at that moment.

    Watch your own noise. Above about 12 kt your bearings get ragged and quiet contacts fade. Contacts in the baffles, 30° either side of dead astern, are lost entirely. Turning briefly smears bearings too.

    DEMON gives blade count and shaft RPM. Divide RPM by the class's turns per knot (TPK) for a speed check on your ruler.

    The torpedo runs at 45 kt to the enable point, then snakes with its seeker on. It acquires anything loud inside about 40° of its nose and 1.5 to 3.5 kyd. Warships and submarines turn away and run when they hear it. While the wire holds (own speed 20 kt or less) you can update its presets from a better solution.
